Slop Mop

Fold or highlight AI-written posts in your LinkedIn feed.

What it does

On LinkedIn, Slop Mop reads posts as they load, asks TypeSafe's Jev model to judge the writing against a library of known AI tells (whether an AI drafted it is one input among twelve), and either folds the worst into a paper strip or outlines them (yellow: possibly slop, red: likely slop). It targets formulaic AI writing, and goes easier on posts people find useful.

Vote on a post

Every post gets a small grey mop icon beside its “…” menu. Choose No, Maybe or Probably (slop). In Highlight mode your vote sets the border (blue, yellow or red); in Hide mode “Probably” hides the post. Your votes are kept in this browser and shared anonymously so others can see how many people flagged a post.

Research purposes only

Slop Mop is an experimental research project and a showcase for Jev. Its judgments are probabilistic and will sometimes be wrong. To score a post, its text is sent to the Slop Mop server and on to TypeSafe's Jev model. Here is what happens to it:

  • The server keeps a small registry so people can benefit from each other's votes: for each post it stores a one-way hash of the text (not the text itself), the post's LinkedIn id when it has one, Jev's scores, engagement counts, when it was seen, and how many people voted No / Maybe / Probably.
  • It does not store author names, and does not keep copies of posts. Your votes are attached to a random install id (never your name or LinkedIn account), and only a salted hash of that id is stored.
  • To run the service and track its cost, the server also logs each check: the time, how large it was, how long Jev took, whether it worked, and the hashed install id. Those logs are kept for 90 days and are visible only to the person running the server.
  • Each install can check up to 250 posts a day. Scores and your votes are also kept in this browser.
  • You can turn Slop Mop off at any time from the toolbar icon.
